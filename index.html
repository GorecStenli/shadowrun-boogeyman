Background / Notes

To the world he’s quiet and almost forgettable until the instant he isn’t — a lean man in a close-cut black suit, dark shirt and tie, a long dark coat over it. Dark hair combed back with a few strands loose, a short careful beard, and eyes that are calm, tired, and very hard to look away from. His augmentations don’t show — the wiring and plating sit seamless under the skin — so people just feel the stillness and never work out why it frightens them. He speaks little, in blunt, heavy Russian-accented English: a man of focus, commitment, and sheer quiet will. They still tell the story about a bar, three men, and a pencil; nobody who laughs at it has met him. Under the sharp clothes rides the kit — a ruthenium cloak beneath the coat, armor beneath that.

Then it starts, and he moves like nothing human should. A street orphan raised by the bratva, years deep in the trade, his body rebuilt for one thing: clearing a room full of armed men and walking out. Wired reflexes that act three times before most men move once; pistols, rifle and bare hands as one thing — shoot, close, grapple, throw, shoot again; laced bones and treated skin that soak a beating and keep coming. He works close and mid; the long shot he leaves to others. A built-in dartgun drops a target quietly when the job wants them breathing. Cold, precise, relentless — he kills when it serves the job or the crew, never for pleasure, never more than needed, but when it’s time nobody in the room is faster. He isn’t the thing under your bed; he’s the one they send to kill it. His one unbreakable loyalty is to his own: he came to Europe to finish his blood-brother Steel’s work and settle the debt, and for the crew he’d die without a word.
